Same word, different cut

A few names mean different things depending on where you're from. These are the ones that cause the most confusion at the counter:

  • Churrasco: in Brazil it means barbecue in general; on Nicaraguan and Cuban menus it means skirt steak.
  • Lomo: in Argentina it's the tenderloin; in Spain it usually means the loin.
  • Pernil: in Puerto Rico it's a roasted pork shoulder; in Brazil it's the leg.
  • Tocino: in Mexico it usually means bacon; in other countries it means pork fat or belly.
  • Falda: in Mexico it's flank steak; in Argentina it's a fattier rib cut used for puchero.

How to order like a pro

  1. Name the dish. "Carne asada for 8," "bistec for the week," or "feijoada on Sunday" tells the butcher more than a cut name.
  2. Give the thickness in inches. 1 inch is about 2.5 cm. Thin bistec is around ¼ inch (6 mm).
  3. Think in pounds. 1 lb is 454 g, and 1 kg is 2.2 lb.
  4. Say how you want the fat. Left on for the grill, or trimmed.
  5. Call ahead for large or unusual orders: (863) 422-2313.
Butcher tipRegional names overlap and every country cuts a little differently. If you're not sure, tell us the name you know and what you're cooking, and we'll help you find the closest match.

Quick answers

What is skirt steak called in Spanish?

Entraña in Argentina, arrachera in Mexico, and churrasco on many Nicaraguan and Cuban menus.

What is picanha called in English?

Top sirloin cap. It is also sold as rump cap or coulotte.
