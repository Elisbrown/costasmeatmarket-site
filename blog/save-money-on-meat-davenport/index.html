How to Save on Quality Meat in Davenport: Bundles, Specials, and WhatsApp Deals

Meat is often the biggest line on the grocery bill. You don't have to buy less of it; you just have to buy it smarter. Here's how to stretch your budget without giving up good cuts.

1. Buy family bundles

Our family combos and BBQ bundles put several cuts together so a week of dinners or a weekend cookout costs less than buying each piece separately. Ask at the counter or check the online store for this week's bundles.

2. Join the WhatsApp VIP group

3. Get 5% off for following us

Follow and engage with us on all of our socials and get 5% off your next purchase. Just show us at the counter. One coupon per customer.

4. Buy bigger pieces and portion them

Larger pieces are often a better deal per pound than individual steaks. Ask what's available, and we can cut it into meal-size portions so you can freeze what you won't cook this week.

5. Cook cuts that eat well and usually cost less

  • Chuck (acém, aguja): braises, stews, and the best burger grind.
  • Chicken thighs and drumsticks: more flavor than breasts and hard to overcook.
  • Pork shoulder: pernil, carnitas, and pulled pork that feed a crowd.
  • Pork chops: quick weeknight dinners on the grill or in the pan.
  • Ground beef: picadillo, burgers, meatballs, and meat sauce.

6. Freeze it right

Buying on special only saves money if nothing goes to waste.

  • Wrap tightly in freezer paper or freezer bags with the air pressed out, or vacuum-seal.
  • Label each pack with the cut and the date.
  • For best quality, the USDA suggests using frozen steaks and roasts within 4–12 months, ground beef within 3–4 months, and chicken pieces within about 9 months.
  • Thaw in the fridge, not on the counter. Steaks thaw overnight; large roasts can take a day or more.

7. Cook once, eat twice

Grill extra chicken or steak and turn the leftovers into tacos, rice bowls, salads, or sandwiches the next day. Cooked leftovers keep 3–4 days in the fridge.
