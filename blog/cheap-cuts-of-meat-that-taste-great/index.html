Cheap Cuts of Meat That Taste Expensive, and Exactly How to Cook Each One

The cheapest cuts are often the most flavorful. They just need the right cooking method. Treat a budget cut like a steak and you'll be disappointed; cook it the right way and nobody will guess what you spent.

The rule: tough cuts love time, thin cuts love heat

Muscles that work hard (shoulder, leg, shank) are full of flavor and connective tissue. Cook them low and slow until the collagen melts, or slice them thin and cook them fast. The middle ground, like a medium-cooked thick piece, is where cheap cuts go wrong.

Five tricks that make cheap cuts taste better

  1. Salt early. Salting a few hours ahead, or overnight, seasons deeply and keeps meat juicy.
  2. Marinate thin cuts. Citrus, garlic and a little oil do wonders for bistec and chops.
  3. Slice against the grain. Short fibers chew tender.
  4. Use the pressure cooker. It turns a 3-hour braise into about an hour.
  5. Ask the butcher to cut it. Thin slices, cubes or a tied roast, cut exactly for your recipe.

Quick answers

What is the cheapest cut of beef that tastes good?

Chuck is a favorite: inexpensive, well marbled and perfect for pot roast and stews. Top round sliced thin and ground beef are also great budget choices.

How do you make cheap meat tender?

Cook tough cuts low and slow until the collagen melts, or slice them thin and cook them fast. Salting ahead, marinating and slicing against the grain all help.
