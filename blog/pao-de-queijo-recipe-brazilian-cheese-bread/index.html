Pão de Queijo: How to Make Brazilian Cheese Bread with Polvilho (Tapioca Starch)

Crisp outside, chewy and cheesy inside: pão de queijo is the Brazilian snack everyone falls for. It's naturally gluten-free and easier to make than it looks. The only trick is the right starch.

The key ingredient: polvilho

Pão de queijo is made with polvilho, a starch extracted from cassava (yuca). There are two kinds:

TypeAlso calledWhat it does
Polvilho azedo (sour)Almidón agrio, sour cassava starchFermented; gives a taller puff, crisp crust and tangy flavor
Polvilho doce (sweet)Tapioca starch / tapioca flourNeutral; gives a softer, chewier, denser bread

Many cooks use half and half. If you only have tapioca flour from the supermarket, it works, but the result is denser. Brazilian brands of polvilho and ready-made pão de queijo mix are part of our grocery shelves; stock changes, so ask at the counter or check our online store.

The cheese

In Brazil it's queijo minas meia-cura or padrão. In the U.S., a mix of finely grated Parmesan (for flavor) and low-moisture mozzarella (for stretch) gets you very close. Grate it fine so it melts into the dough.

Pão de queijo (Brazilian cheese bread)

  • Serves About 24 pieces
  • Prep 25 min
  • Cook 25 min
  • Total 1 hr

Ingredients

  • 2 cups (250 g) polvilho azedo, or half polvilho azedo and half polvilho doce
  • 1 cup (240 ml) whole milk
  • ½ cup (120 ml) vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1½ cups (150 g) finely grated cheese: half Parmesan, half low-moisture mozzarella

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 400 °F and line a baking sheet with parchment.
  2. Put the polvilho in a large bowl. Bring the milk, oil and salt to a boil in a small pot.
  3. Pour the hot milk over the polvilho and mix with a spoon until it forms a crumbly, sticky dough. Let it cool until warm to the touch, about 10 minutes.
  4. Add the eggs one at a time, mixing well after each, until the dough is smooth and shiny.
  5. Mix in the cheese and knead briefly by hand until even. The dough should be soft and hold its shape.
  6. With oiled hands, roll balls about 1½ inches wide and set them 2 inches apart on the sheet.
  7. Bake for 25 to 30 minutes, until puffed and golden. Serve warm.

Tips and fixes

  • Dough too runny? Add polvilho a spoonful at a time. Too dry and cracking? Add a little milk.
  • Oil your hands before rolling; the dough is sticky.
  • Freeze for later: freeze the raw balls on a tray, then bag them. Bake straight from frozen, adding 5 to 10 minutes.
  • Using a mix? Follow the package; most just need eggs, milk or water, and oil.

Serve it with

Coffee in the morning, or as a starter at a churrasco while the coals heat up. Looking for more Brazilian staples? See our guide to Brazilian groceries in Davenport.

Quick answers

What flour is used for pão de queijo?

Polvilho, a cassava starch. Polvilho azedo (sour) gives a crisp, airy bread; polvilho doce, sold in the U.S. as tapioca starch, gives a softer, chewier one.

Is pão de queijo gluten-free?

Yes. It's made with cassava starch, cheese, eggs, milk and oil, with no wheat. Check the labels of any mix or cheese you use.
